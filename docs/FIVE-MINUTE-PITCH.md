## English script

### Introduction

**00:00–00:12**

> Your morning should belong to you.
> I'm Guillaume, and we're building MORE to take care of your morning routine.
> Let's start with what happens after the alarm.

### Problem

**00:12–00:46**

> UK adults spend an average of four and a half hours online each day.
> We focus on one familiar part of that daily habit: checking messages, calendars, news and social feeds while trying to get ready for the day.
> What if you could keep the parts you enjoy and let your phone handle the repetitive steps?

Preparation source: [Ofcom Online Nation 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025).

### Solution

**00:46–01:16**

> MORE's goal is to carry out the routine you have approved.
> You choose the useful information and enjoyable moments you want to keep, and MORE is designed to handle those steps with voice interaction while you get ready.
> First, it needs to understand what your own morning looks like.

### Learning and approval

**01:16–01:36**

> Learning only matters when you can correct it.
> MORE is designed to turn chosen Screen Time data, recordings and browser use into a routine you can edit and approve.
> That approval prepares the next morning.

### Introduce the demo

**01:36–01:51**

> Tomorrow, the routine should already be ready.
> This is a recording of our prototype, with the full hands-free journey still under validation.
> Let's watch what the morning could feel like.

### Demo

**01:51–02:58:** say nothing; let the original sound play. The routine runs until 02:32, the social sequence until 02:55, and the audio ending continues afterwards. **02:58–03:00:** pause, face the audience, prepare the market sentence.

### Market and competitors

**03:00–03:25**

> Opal's founder reports having reached ten million dollars in annual recurring revenue.
> Opal and one sec help people control app use, while Siri and Shortcuts already automate tasks.
> Our hypothesis is a dedicated morning experience that learns your routine, lets you approve it, and then carries it out.

Preparation sources: [Opal founder statement](https://opalapp.com/blog/screen-time-next-big-category), [one sec](https://one-sec.app/faq/), [Apple Shortcuts](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iph47e1c9d7d/ios).

### First customers

**03:25–03:45**

> Our first users have a routine they already repeat.
> We will begin with iPhone users who check several services each morning, recruiting through our own network and focused user communities.
> The first test is whether they return and choose to pay.

### Business model

**03:45–04:05**

> Our planned subscription is €4.99 a month, with a one-week trial for eligible customers.
> Free entry remains available, with the paid offer built around reliable ongoing routines.
> That makes reliability and trust essential to the business.

Preparation source: [Current subscription decisions](MONETIZATION.md). The commercial offer and paid packaging still need live purchase and customer validation.

### Architecture and trust

**04:05–04:35**

> Trust has to come before delegation.
> Our design keeps routine learning local by default, makes the plan visible before approval, and gives the person control to pause or stop.
> Our next milestone is to prove the complete experience repeatedly with real users on their own iPhones, before extending it further.

### Future and closing

**04:35–05:00**

> Morning is only the beginning.
> The final scene illustrates a future direction for MORE: a trusted assistant carrying the routine while you keep control of your day.
> We call it MORE, because the best thing your phone could give you is more of your own life.

## What to say and what not to say

- **Say / Dire:** “a recorded prototype” / « un prototype enregistré ». **Avoid / Éviter:** “live autonomous operation across every app” / « une autonomie en direct dans toutes les apps ». A recording does not prove provider effects or uninterrupted hands-free execution.
- **Say / Dire:** “designed to learn, review and execute an approved routine” / « conçu pour apprendre, faire valider puis exécuter une routine ». **Avoid / Éviter:** “it already does everything you just saw automatically” / « tout ce que vous voyez est déjà automatique ».
- **Say / Dire:** “local learning by default” / « apprentissage local par défaut ». **Avoid / Éviter:** “nothing ever leaves the phone” / « rien ne quitte jamais le téléphone »; connected services have their own data flows.
- **Say / Dire:** “we will validate paid repeat use” / « nous allons valider un usage payant récurrent ». **Avoid / Éviter:** invented customers, revenue, savings, conversion or retention / chiffres de clients, revenus, économies ou rétention inventés.
- **Say / Dire:** “a future MORE experience on this form factor” / « une direction future pour MORE sur ce format ». **Avoid / Éviter:** any claim of a working Duo integration, Apple partnership or hardware built by MORE / intégration Duo démontrée, partenariat Apple ou matériel développé par MORE.
- **Say / Dire:** “focused morning experience” / « expérience dédiée au matin ». **Avoid / Éviter:** “the first”, “the only”, “Siri cannot automate”, or “all competitors only block apps” / « le premier », « le seul », « Siri n'automatise rien », « tous les concurrents ne font que bloquer ».

## Business model for questions

**Offer.** Free app entry plus a planned optional **€4.99 monthly subscription**, with a one-week trial where eligible. The precise paid benefit and live purchase flow must be validated before treating the offer as launched. The current decision is in [MONETIZATION.md](MONETIZATION.md); older alternative price experiments in [BUSINESS.md](BUSINESS.md) are historical hypotheses.

**Why pay monthly?** Reliable recurring preparation and execution should deliver a useful result each morning. Validate willingness to pay through informed purchases and renewal, not a stated interview preference. Do not describe basic privacy controls or deletion as premium features.

**Acquisition.** Start with founder-led recruitment and targeted communities, learn which repeated morning job matters, then test referrals after people voluntarily return. Paid advertising is a later experiment once retention and the cost to serve are understood; no acquisition cost is claimed.

**Three operating metrics.** Track confirmed successful routines, paying-user retention and cost per successful routine. The cost numerator includes failed attempts; a timer completion alone does not establish that intended external effects succeeded. Targets and sample sizes remain planning decisions, not results.

**Fees and costs.** Apple's Small Business Program offers **15% commission** to enrolled, qualifying developers, with an aggregated proceeds threshold of **US$1M** and associated-account rules. Eligibility is not established here. Consumer receipts must also cover applicable taxes, refunds, inference, provider access, voice, hosting and support; 85% is not a profit margin. [Official Apple program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/).

**Retention risk.** RevenueCat's 2026 report gives median 12-month retention of **6.1% for monthly AI subscriptions**, versus **9.5% for non-AI subscriptions** in its dataset. This is a reason to prioritize repeat value, not a forecast for MORE. [RevenueCat report](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).

**No invented market valuation.** Opal's revenue is adjacent-market evidence, not MORE's TAM. A defensible bottom-up estimate still needs an eligible customer count, observed paid conversion and retention; a broad smartphone population multiplied by the planned price would hide those unknowns.
